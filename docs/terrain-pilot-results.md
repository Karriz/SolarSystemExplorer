# Terrain/material milestone: initial pilot

Updated: 2026-10-30. Status: Earth follow-up implemented; full quality/hardware acceptance remains open.

The pilot establishes bounded Earth/Moon terrain delivery, a shared procedural material path, dynamic lighting, and reproducible data preparation. Earth now streams sharper regional color and view-centered coverage; seamless source boundaries, calibrated reflectance, and hardware performance remain unaccepted. The [upgrade plan](terrain-upgrade-plan.md) retains those gates.

## Confirmed spike cause: privacy-protected canvas readback

The repeated pyramids were reproduced in **Brave on `http://localhost:5173/`**. They were not explained by Mapterhorn's nominal source accuracy. The old worker used `createImageBitmap → OffscreenCanvas → getImageData` to obtain numerical elevation channels. Brave deliberately perturbs canvas readback for fingerprinting protection. In Terrarium encoding, a red-channel least-significant-bit change alters height by **256 m**; bilinear resampling spreads that error into isolated mesh peaks or pits. Different tile/LOD content produces different perturbed pixels, explaining the apparent movement as the camera changes the selected tiles.

Independent Rasterio/GDAL decoding gave the Finnish fixture a height range of 33.75–72 m. Brave's canvas readback of those same compressed bytes produced values up to 325 m in one captured run. Byte comparisons in another Brave run found 248 changed Finnish pixels and 242 changed Alpine pixels, with maximum height errors of 256 m in both. The direct decoder matched the independent RGBA SHA-256 references exactly.

The production worker now decodes the WebP bytes with **libwebp WebAssembly**, without canvas, bitmap readback, WebGL readback, or browser-specific detection. Shields remains enabled. The initial correction retained 65×65 height resampling; the follow-up below increases Earth worker grids to 129×129. Source resolution and the zoom-15 cap remain unchanged. There is no canvas fallback for numerical heights.

An identical-view Brave comparison restored the old worker only through a test route: its cached fine-LOD grids had 81 isolated residuals exceeding 40 m; the corrected worker had zero. The before/after screenshots reproduced and removed the narrow pyramids. Prior edge/shader experiments did not fix this root cause, and Chromium-only validation missed the browser-specific corruption.

Validation now includes 64 automated tests, two real Finnish/Alpine tile fixtures with independent decoded-pixel checksums, forbidden-canvas/bitmap tests, and a one-bit-noise regression that reproduces the height spike. `npm run terrain:check-decoder -- --executable /path/to/brave --url http://localhost:5173` checks exact pixels and the actual worker in Brave without changing privacy settings. The check requires the Vite dev server. The decoder/worker check passed in both Brave and Chromium. A production preview in Brave loaded the hashed WASM asset with HTTP 200 and rendered the Alps without page errors or failed terrain requests. The production build emits a hashed 137.96 kB decoder WASM asset (49.49 kB gzip); it is loaded once on first elevation decode, with no offline terrain rebuild or extra terrain payloads.

References: [Brave fingerprinting defenses](https://brave.com/privacy-updates/4-fingerprinting-defenses-2.0/), [Brave canvas implementation](https://github.com/brave/brave-core/blob/master/chromium_src/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/canvas/canvas2d/base_rendering_context_2d.cc), [jSquash WebP decoder](https://github.com/jamsinclair/jSquash/tree/main/packages/webp). Fixture provenance and attribution are in `tests/fixtures/mapterhorn/README.md`.

## Historical Earth rendering simplification

Before the follow-up below, the initial edge-stitch attempt did **not** resolve the spikes reported in both the Alps and Finland. At that stage Earth rendered decoded Mapterhorn heights with a plain standard material and normals derived from the mesh. The procedural surface shader, raster-derived slope-normal override, classification controls, and focused terrain shadows were disabled for Earth. Focused shadows are now restored as described below. Lunar material experiments remain separate.

Earth's streamed imagery shell was also disabled: its independent tessellation overlapped the base sphere and produced globe-scale triangular artifacts. The bundled Blue Marble base remains the color source. Regional elevation overlays switch off above 1.05 Earth radii (roughly 319 km altitude). Direct tile requests are capped at zoom 15; a Finnish zoom-15 request succeeded and the corresponding zoom-16 request returned 404. TileJSON does not declare a maximum zoom.

A real Finnish zoom-14 tile decoded to 33.75–72 m with opaque pixels and no local four-neighbor residual over 20 m. A sampled Alpine zoom-14 tile ranged from 2,785.75–4,023.25 m; its largest local residual was approximately 25 m. These spot checks support inspecting the rendering path rather than filtering away measured peaks. They are not whole-dataset accuracy validation.

Browser checks covered tilted Finland at 500 m, tilted Alps starting at 1,000 m, and Earth at 4,000 km altitude. The checked views showed no isolated pyramids or overlapping globe triangles, no page/shader errors, and tracked regional allocations stayed below 48 MiB. No GIBS Earth-shell or WorldCover-control requests occurred. At that stage the automated suite had 62 tests, including plain Earth materials/normals, far-view fallback, and excluded overlapping imagery groups. Earlier material/performance findings below describe the initial prototype and are not current Earth acceptance evidence.

## Earth LOD, color, and coverage follow-up

The following replaces the three-area/plain-color delivery described in the historical checks above:

- **Geometry:** decode native 512×512 Terrarium WebP directly with libwebp WASM; retain 129×129 geographic heights. Render 33×33 grids at zooms 8–10, 65×65 at 11–13, and 129×129 at 14–15. At latitude 60.38°, zoom-15 mesh spacing is approximately 4.7 m, compared with approximately 9.4 m for the former 65 grid at the same zoom. These are mesh spacings, not source accuracy guarantees. Full 512-pixel meshes and zoom-16 requests are not needed.
- **Selection:** split/retain thresholds of 2.5/1.5 projected error pixels, surface-only culling bounds excluding skirts, at most 32 visible leaves and 400,000 surface-plus-skirt triangles. Parent meshes remain until all four replacement children are loaded. Mixed-resolution edges share the actual 3D polyline. Heights are neither procedurally displaced nor filtered to suppress legitimate peaks. There is no explicit interior-height geomorph yet.
- **Memory:** four elevation requests, a 48 MiB estimated terrain cache, retained decoded heights with hidden GPU geometry evicted under pressure, local parent rebuild on zoom-out, and eventual eviction of old decoded tiles. Terrain under the camera is retained/picked independently of screen visibility during tilt. Counters exclude total browser/driver/worker overhead.
- **Coverage:** four native zoom-8 roots follow the point on the ground in view, with longitude wrapping. Finland/Alps/Namibia buttons are bookmarks. New York also loaded native terrain and color in Brave. Native elevation outside ±85.051° latitude, unavailable fine tiles, and altitude above approximately 319 km retain the globe fallback. This is provider-backed streaming rather than a newly stored worldwide dataset.
- **Color:** direct 256×256 PNG tiles from Terrascope's `esa-worldcover-s2rgbnir-10m-2021-v2_tcc` WMTS, time `2021-01-01`, EPSG:3857, zooms 8–14. Use KVP `SERVICE=WMTS&REQUEST=GetTile&VERSION=1.0.0`; the advertised REST pattern failed the actual access test. The working KVP route returned HTTP 200 and appropriate browser CORS on localhost. A sampled zoom-14 tile was about 37 KB. No preprocessing, mirrored archive, private API key, or application imagery server is introduced.
- **Color budgets/fallback:** three requests and 32 MiB estimated retained bitmap plus GPU mip allocations, independently capped at zoom 14. Cached parent color remains usable; request order prioritizes finer, view-centered tiles. Obsolete work aborts; 30-second timeout/backoff retains bundled Blue Marble. Provider extent is approximately 60°S–83°N; alpha/NoData also falls back. Color is draped onto the measured mesh, not a separate sphere. Visible partial/coarse color can remain while the external service is slow. A nominal 10 m composite remains visibly pixelated at 100 m camera clearance.
- **Lighting:** focused Earth/Moon 1024² shadow map, skirt-excluding depth pass, **Shadows on/off**, contribution fading between 30 and 50 km altitude. Earth still uses geometry-derived normals, with procedural grain/classification/raster-slope overrides disabled. Sentinel-2 RGB contains acquisition lighting; adding dynamic shadows does not make it physically calibrated, shadow-free albedo.

A Brave 156.1.98.62 localhost check at 100 m above Finland reached zoom 15 with six 129×129 visible grids, 202,752 terrain triangles, 44,462,112 tracked terrain bytes, and 8,563,380 tracked color bytes. Elevation/color requests settled with zero failures. Tilting retained approximately 100 m terrain clearance and stayed below both cache limits and the triangle cap. An earlier settled 12 km Alps check reached zoom 13 with 30 visible 65×65 grids and 261,120 triangles. These are view-specific development measurements, not hardware performance acceptance; the browser uses SwiftShader.

The final Brave workflow also exercised shadow on/off at desktop 1280×900, phone 390×844, and landscape 844×390, including Alps illumination at approximately 12° Sun elevation. At 4,000 km altitude, regional meshes and requests were inactive and the globe had no triangular overlap artifacts. Blocking the hosted RGB endpoint left New York terrain visible with bundled color fallback. Switching to Hadley loaded lunar relief and reduced both Earth terrain and imagery allocations to zero. No page/shader errors were recorded. The Alps color queue was still active in some captures, so those screenshots are not a fully settled imagery-quality acceptance. Development logs are in the ignored `.terrain-cache/pilot/browser-earth-followup/` directory.

The automated suite now has **71 tests**, including adaptive sample preservation, cross-density edge welding, dateline/polar root selection, triangle/allocation limits, parent-geometry rebuild without redownload, Mercator imagery mapping, color fallback/backoff, and late decode/disposal. The independent Finnish/Alpine pixel fixtures and Brave decoder regression remain required. The final Brave check matched both independent RGBA hashes and produced 16,641 finite height samples per worker tile; canvas readback still differed by up to 256 m, confirming the numerical path remains isolated from privacy noise. The lean production build also loaded the hashed WASM and hosted PNGs with HTTP 200 in Brave, rendered terrain, and reported no page/shader errors (asset/shader smoke test during refinement, not a settled quality benchmark). Existing Moon/Mars/Mercury/Venus global terrain releases are unchanged.

## What is available

Select Earth or Moon, then a location under **Explore measured terrain**. Earth offers southern Finland, the Bernese Alps, and Brandberg in Namibia. The Moon offers Hadley–Apennine. On the Moon, **Surface detail on/off** compares the same measured geometry and regional color with filtered grain/normal/roughness detail enabled or disabled. Earth has no procedural detail toggle. Procedural grain is appearance, not observed rocks or additional measured relief.

Earth streams native 512-pixel Mapterhorn Terrarium WebP tiles around the view. Workers preserve numeric channels and resample to 129×129 geographic heights; adaptive meshes use 33/65/129 rows as described above. Earth surface normals now come from the rendered triangles; the earlier delivered-raster slope override is disabled. Presets are inside the coverage footprints rather than directly against their edges. Earth remains a mean-radius sphere; ellipsoid/geoid alignment and coastal water treatment are not accepted yet. The prototype's direct XYZ access was checked with real browser requests and CORS; there is no service uptime/throughput guarantee or mirrored offline Earth dataset.

The lunar overlay uses measured SLDEM2015 heights at approximately 118.45 m sampling, obtained by averaging the approximately 59 m source grid by two. The finite hierarchy has 341 tiles, geographic levels 6–10, a 1,737,400 m reference radius, and the documented ME/PA DE421 frame. Heights use half-meter Int16 steps. Shared source-derived east/north gradients keep surface normals consistent across LODs; half-meter quantization is storage precision, not elevation accuracy. Existing LOLA remains the outer fallback.

Each regional layer has four concurrent requests, a 32-leaf selection limit, and a 48 MiB estimated terrain allocation budget. Parents remain until all four children are ready. Failed requests back off, obsolete requests abort, and changing bodies releases regional geometry. These limits are separate from global terrain and imagery caches. Shadows use one focused 1024² map, with crack-hiding skirts excluded from shadow casting.

The lunar pilot has a bounded flat regional manifest (maximum 4,096 entries and 512,000 bytes). This is intentionally separate from the existing complete-global contract. Large/deep overlays still require hierarchical metadata; raising the existing global depth limit is not the implementation strategy.

## Rendering findings and limits

Earth's bundled base and streamed Blue Marble layer now omit added shaded relief. Lunar WAC morphological imagery is suppressed in favor of the existing normalized CGI Moon Kit color. These are practical rendering inputs, not claims of perfectly calibrated, entirely unlit albedo. Other bodies retain their previous display composites.

The initial regional pilot sampled the existing global color texture on its geometry. Earth now adds the hosted composite above; the Moon retains its global color. Initially that color was visibly coarse at medium/close distance; Earth vegetation/canopy, roads, shorelines, and lunar local albedo features remain source-limited. Classification controls fine material response rather than replacing the geographic color. Two inferred surface families and a Finnish WorldCover mask validate the path; a complete biome/material library has not been built. Water labels suppress grain, but this is not a full shoreline or water-elevation solution.

Mercator latitude and longitude spans are handled independently. Regional vertices use tile-local origins before Float32 conversion, preserving sub-millimeter placement in the tested close Earth patch. Body-fixed, periodic procedural coordinates likewise avoid large-coordinate precision loss. Detail fades by pixel footprint and does not displace measured geometry.

Source-boundary height blending remains unfinished. Skirts cover LOD cracks but cannot make a terrain-to-sphere or SLDEM-to-LOLA height mismatch physically continuous. Do not use the pilot edge as an accepted geographic transition. The initial rugged-terrain comparison showed mixed-LOD lighting/height transitions. Regional tile edges now share a deterministic 3D border and interpolated normals: fine edges follow the actual coarse polyline, including its spherical chord. Skirt bottoms move with the corrected tops, preventing them from becoming upward wedges after a large height adjustment. Original edge samples are restored when neighbors change, and measured interior vertices remain unchanged. This removes the independent-edge mismatch; neighboring raster halos, wider continuity checks, and outer-source blending remain acceptance work. Source uncertainty remains distinct from the sampled mesh approximation error.

## Validation evidence

- 60 automated tests cover existing camera/terrain behavior plus numeric Terrarium resampling, sparse manifest validation, regional parent replacement, cancellation/backoff, shared-edge normals, tile-local precision, checksummed three-plane worker decoding, and equal/mixed-LOD border stitching with skirt-depth and restoration checks.
- TypeScript and the lean production build pass. Existing global source releases are retained; no global rebuild is required for the pilot.
- Chromium rendered three Earth locations, lunar terrain, and a 100 m lunar view at desktop 1280×900, phone 390×844, and landscape 844×390 sizes. The initial nine-case run produced no page/shader errors. An aborted Earth endpoint retained fallback, and switching to the Moon released Earth allocations and loaded measured lunar terrain.
- Follow-up checks cover the corrected Mercator geometry, tile-local shader, a 100 m Finnish view, and lunar illumination at approximately 45° Sun elevation. Crack-hiding skirts were removed from the shadow depth pass after visual review. Final focused checks also clicked location/detail controls at phone and landscape sizes, with no page/shader errors. In a 32×32 center sample at 100 m on the Moon, toggling detail changed mean RGB from 164.33 to 164.53 on the 0–255 display scale (mean absolute pixel difference 0.57); this is one local check, not a global color-stability proof.

The initial nine-case report/screenshots are in `.terrain-cache/pilot/browser/`, generated by `npm run terrain:benchmark`. That run predates the final Mercator-span/precision corrections, Earth raster-normal changes, and preset placement adjustments; its numbers are a prototype cost record, not final acceptance measurements. Its original `settleMs` also included screenshot/frame measurement time; the script now stops that timer immediately after settling.

The test machine uses Chromium 151 and **ANGLE SwiftShader**, a software WebGL renderer. Initial detail-on p95 RAF intervals ranged from approximately 83 to 567 ms, exceeding the proposed desktop 16.7 ms/mobile 33.3 ms goals. A phone-sized viewport on this machine is not a real mobile GPU test. Hardware performance remains unaccepted.

Initial tracked regional allocations were approximately 17–45 MiB for Earth across successive locations and 19–21 MiB for the Moon; settled views selected up to 31 patches. These counters include estimated mesh CPU/GPU arrays and retained height/slope arrays, plus a reservation for in-flight requests. They exclude full browser/worker overhead, decoded imagery, texture allocations, shadow maps, and global terrain. Transfer/decode/build counters are cumulative per body, including earlier locations and potentially discarded requests. They are not a complete network trace or per-view total memory benchmark.

## Tile-edge spike correction

The Alps spike report prompted tilted-view checks with shadows enabled/disabled and inspection of mesh borders. Independently sampled borders could disagree, exposing crack-hiding skirts as narrow wedges. The regional renderer now welds visible tile borders in coarse-to-fine order and keeps skirt bottoms below their corrected tops. Equal-level borders have a deterministic owner. The pass reruns only when the visible tile set or exaggeration changes; its CPU time is included in the build counter.

Original perimeter data uses approximately 11 KiB per 65×65 patch, scaling with the adaptive mesh perimeter, in the estimated allocation counter. Published terrain payloads and measured interior heights are unchanged. Regression tests deliberately introduce a greater-than-skirt-depth mismatch, check the shared 3D edge to within 5 mm, preserve interior samples, and restore source edges after removing a neighbor. Outer pilot-to-fallback boundaries remain separate work.

## Next acceptance work

1. Broaden medium-distance color registration comparisons with fixed cameras/lighting; distinguish acquisition shadows in the hosted RGB from dynamically generated shading. Compare suitable reflectance alternatives only if the composite limits become a visible problem.
2. Blend source footprints and establish Earth datum/coastal handling; verify tilted views and traversing the coverage boundary during streaming.
3. Measure combined peak memory, full network sequences, conversion RSS, and frame times on named desktop/mobile hardware. Tune LOD, request/cache limits, and shadows from those results.
4. Complete identical camera-path, neutral-material, low-Sun/multiple-azimuth, and close-detail stability comparisons. Keep future offline planetary expansion gated on visible benefit and resource measurements; current Earth coverage uses bounded native streaming.

Run/preparation/build instructions are in the [README](../README.md). Deeper global metadata, meter-scale destinations, geometric scattering, and new Mars/Mercury/Venus processing remain later scope.
