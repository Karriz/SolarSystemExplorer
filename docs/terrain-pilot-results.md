# Terrain/material milestone: initial pilot

Updated: 2026-11-01. Status: global Moon elevation and Earth follow-ups implemented; full quality/hardware acceptance remains open.

The pilot establishes bounded Earth/Moon terrain delivery, a shared procedural material path, dynamic lighting, and reproducible data preparation. Earth now streams sharper regional color and view-centered coverage; seamless source boundaries, calibrated reflectance, and hardware performance remain unaccepted. The [upgrade plan](terrain-upgrade-plan.md) retains those gates.

## Confirmed spike cause: privacy-protected canvas readback

The repeated pyramids were reproduced in **Brave on `http://localhost:5173/`**. They were not explained by Mapterhorn's nominal source accuracy. The old worker used `createImageBitmap → OffscreenCanvas → getImageData` to obtain numerical elevation channels. Brave deliberately perturbs canvas readback for fingerprinting protection. In Terrarium encoding, a red-channel least-significant-bit change alters height by **256 m**; bilinear resampling spreads that error into isolated mesh peaks or pits. Different tile/LOD content produces different perturbed pixels, explaining the apparent movement as the camera changes the selected tiles.

Independent Rasterio/GDAL decoding gave the Finnish fixture a height range of 33.75–72 m. Brave's canvas readback of those same compressed bytes produced values up to 325 m in one captured run. Byte comparisons in another Brave run found 248 changed Finnish pixels and 242 changed Alpine pixels, with maximum height errors of 256 m in both. The direct decoder matched the independent RGBA SHA-256 references exactly.

The production worker now decodes the WebP bytes with **libwebp WebAssembly**, without canvas, bitmap readback, WebGL readback, or browser-specific detection. Shields remains enabled. The initial correction retained 65×65 height resampling; the follow-up below increases Earth worker grids to 129×129. Source resolution and the zoom-15 cap remain unchanged. There is no canvas fallback for numerical heights.

An identical-view Brave comparison restored the old worker only through a test route: its cached fine-LOD grids had 81 isolated residuals exceeding 40 m; the corrected worker had zero. The before/after screenshots reproduced and removed the narrow pyramids. Prior edge/shader experiments did not fix this root cause, and Chromium-only validation missed the browser-specific corruption.

Validation now includes 64 automated tests, two real Finnish/Alpine tile fixtures with independent decoded-pixel checksums, forbidden-canvas/bitmap tests, and a one-bit-noise regression that reproduces the height spike. `npm run terrain:check-decoder -- --executable /path/to/brave --url http://localhost:5173` checks exact pixels and the actual worker in Brave without changing privacy settings. The check requires the Vite dev server. The decoder/worker check passed in both Brave and Chromium. A production preview in Brave loaded the hashed WASM asset with HTTP 200 and rendered the Alps without page errors or failed terrain requests. The production build emits a hashed 137.96 kB decoder WASM asset (49.49 kB gzip); it is loaded once on first elevation decode, with no offline terrain rebuild or extra terrain payloads.

References: [Brave fingerprinting defenses](https://brave.com/privacy-updates/4-fingerprinting-defenses-2.0/), [Brave canvas implementation](https://github.com/brave/brave-core/blob/master/chromium_src/third_party/blink/renderer/modules/canvas/canvas2d/base_rendering_context_2d.cc), [jSquash WebP decoder](https://github.com/jamsinclair/jSquash/tree/main/packages/webp). Fixture provenance and attribution are in `tests/fixtures/mapterhorn/README.md`.

## Earth LOD, color, and coverage follow-up

## What is available

Select Earth or Moon, then a location under **Explore measured terrain**. Earth offers southern Finland, the Bernese Alps, and Brandberg in Namibia. The Moon offers Hadley–Apennine. On the Moon, **Surface detail on/off** compares the same measured geometry and regional color with filtered grain/normal/roughness detail enabled or disabled. Earth has no procedural detail toggle. Procedural grain is appearance, not observed rocks or additional measured relief.

Earth streams native 512-pixel Mapterhorn Terrarium WebP tiles around the view. Workers preserve numeric channels and resample to 129×129 geographic heights; adaptive meshes use 33/65/129 rows as described above. Earth surface normals now come from the rendered triangles; the earlier delivered-raster slope override is disabled. Presets are inside the coverage footprints rather than directly against their edges. Earth remains a mean-radius sphere; ellipsoid/geoid alignment and coastal water treatment are not accepted yet. The prototype's direct XYZ access was checked with real browser requests and CORS; there is no service uptime/throughput guarantee or mirrored offline Earth dataset.

The lunar overlay uses measured SLDEM2015 heights at approximately 118.45 m sampling, obtained by averaging the approximately 59 m source grid by two. The finite hierarchy has 341 tiles, geographic levels 6–10, a 1,737,400 m reference radius, and the documented ME/PA DE421 frame. Heights use half-meter Int16 steps. Shared source-derived east/north gradients keep surface normals consistent across LODs; half-meter quantization is storage precision, not elevation accuracy. Existing LOLA remains the outer fallback.

Each regional layer has four concurrent requests, a 32-leaf selection limit, and a 48 MiB estimated terrain allocation budget. Parents remain until all four children are ready. Failed requests back off, obsolete requests abort, and changing bodies releases regional geometry. These limits are separate from global terrain and imagery caches. Shadows use one focused 1024² map, with crack-hiding skirts excluded from shadow casting.

The lunar pilot has a bounded flat regional manifest (maximum 4,096 entries and 512,000 bytes). This is intentionally separate from the existing complete-global contract. Large/deep overlays still require hierarchical metadata; raising the existing global depth limit is not the implementation strategy.

## Rendering findings and limits

Earth's bundled base and streamed Blue Marble layer now omit added shaded relief. Lunar WAC morphological imagery is suppressed in favor of the existing normalized CGI Moon Kit color. These are practical rendering inputs, not claims of perfectly calibrated, entirely unlit albedo. Other bodies retain their previous display composites.

The initial regional pilot sampled the existing global color texture on its geometry. Earth now adds the hosted composite above; the Moon retains its global color. Initially that color was visibly coarse at medium/close distance; Earth vegetation/canopy, roads, shorelines, and lunar local albedo features remain source-limited. Classification controls fine material response rather than replacing the geographic color. Two inferred surface families and a Finnish WorldCover mask validate the path; a complete biome/material library has not been built. Water labels suppress grain, but this is not a full shoreline or water-elevation solution.

Mercator latitude and longitude spans are handled independently. Regional vertices use tile-local origins before Float32 conversion, preserving sub-millimeter placement in the tested close Earth patch. Body-fixed, periodic procedural coordinates likewise avoid large-coordinate precision loss. Detail fades by pixel footprint and does not displace measured geometry.

Source-boundary height blending remains unfinished. Skirts cover LOD cracks but cannot make a terrain-to-sphere or SLDEM-to-LOLA height mismatch physically continuous. Do not use the pilot edge as an accepted geographic transition. The initial rugged-terrain comparison showed mixed-LOD lighting/height transitions. Regional tile edges now share a deterministic 3D border and interpolated normals: fine edges follow the actual coarse polyline, including its spherical chord. Skirt bottoms move with the corrected tops, preventing them from becoming upward wedges after a large height adjustment. Original edge samples are restored when neighbors change, and measured interior vertices remain unchanged. This removes the independent-edge mismatch; neighboring raster halos, wider continuity checks, and outer-source blending remain acceptance work. Source uncertainty remains distinct from the sampled mesh approximation error.

The Alps spike report prompted tilted-view checks with shadows enabled/disabled and inspection of mesh borders. Independently sampled borders could disagree, exposing crack-hiding skirts as narrow wedges. The regional renderer now welds visible tile borders in coarse-to-fine order and keeps skirt bottoms below their corrected tops. Equal-level borders have a deterministic owner. The pass reruns only when the visible tile set or exaggeration changes; its CPU time is included in the build counter.

Original perimeter data uses approximately 11 KiB per 65×65 patch, scaling with the adaptive mesh perimeter, in the estimated allocation counter. Published terrain payloads and measured interior heights are unchanged. Regression tests deliberately introduce a greater-than-skirt-depth mismatch, check the shared 3D edge to within 5 mm, preserve interior samples, and restore source edges after removing a neighbor. ### Earth height and coast policy

Continue to interpret provider heights as radial offsets from the existing 6,371,000 m mean-radius sphere. This is a visualization convention, not a WGS84 ellipsoid/geoid transformation. Neither the [provider access contract](https://mapterhorn.com/data-access/) nor its tile encoding establishes a uniform vertical datum for every contributing source; an [upstream vertical-reference discussion](https://github.com/mapterhorn/mapterhorn/issues/255) explicitly raises this issue. Do not apply a guessed global EGM96/EGM2008 offset. A future ellipsoid conversion requires source-specific height references and a consistent change to geometry, picking, camera safety, and the fallback globe.

## Confirmed imagery-band cause and correction — 2026-10-31

This supersedes the unresolved horizontal-band finding above. An identical-view A/B test retained the bands when terrain skirts were removed. CPU reconstruction of the old geographic-to-image mapping stayed inside the correct tile bounds. A separate WebGL transform-feedback test then measured the old shader's actual projected coordinates: for a Finnish zoom-14 tile, the expected north-to-south 1–0 range was approximately **0.81445 to −0.18457** on SwiftShader. This projection error pushed image lookup outside the tile, where edge clamping stretched the last image row into a band. The problem was GPU projection arithmetic, not measured elevation or evidence that the provider RGB needed repainting. Other GPU drivers were not measured.

Earth now computes Web Mercator image coordinates in CPU double precision when building geometry and uploads tile-local UVs. Parent-image scale/offset is also resolved on the CPU. The vertex shader performs only a small local affine transform; it no longer evaluates globe-scale `tan`/`log` for imagery. Geographic UVs remain available for bundled/alpha fallback, and skirt bottoms copy the surface perimeter's image UVs. The extra two-float attribute is counted by the existing CPU/GPU geometry budget; no terrain dataset or measured height is changed.

`npm run terrain:check-color-uv` runs an offscreen WebGL transform-feedback regression against the actual material shader, without hosted tiles or the globe scene. It covers five latitudes/locations (Finland, Alps, Namibia, and ±85°), zooms 8/14/15, and native plus two-level-parent imagery: 30 combinations. All passed on Chromium/SwiftShader; the largest measured error was below **0.00001 image pixel**. CPU regressions check tile boundaries, interior projection, and parent subrectangles. The automated suite passes **75 tests**, and the lean production build passes.

Identical 800×600 Finland views at 12 km clearance show the bands removed with normal skirts enabled. Ignored artifacts are in `.terrain-cache/pilot/band-isolation/`: `original-before.png`, `original-no-skirts.png`, and `fixed.png`. The neighboring root view, surface drag, horizon tilt, and phone-width hosted-imagery outage are checked separately below. This fixes the confirmed image-mapping defect; broader source-footprint blending, datum/shoreline alignment, and real-device performance remain independent work.

## Source-edge continuity and physical ground picking — 2026-10-31
